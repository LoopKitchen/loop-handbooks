Self-serve billing, invoicing, and era history

How Loop bills restaurant chains: the account and structure model, how invoices are generated and delivered, how a live chain changes its billing structure without losing history, and the guard rails that keep money safe, with deep dives into the wizard's state machine, the account resolver, ACH verification, and bulk import. Written from the system as shipped on the grouping-eras branches; flows verified end-to-end on the acme_14 test chain.

The system

Mental model

A chain (one restaurant group, e.g. acme_14) is billed through one or more billing accounts. How its billable locations split across accounts is its billing structure. Because invoices, cards, and usage attach to accounts, a structure change on a live chain never deletes anything: old accounts are closed in place and a new set is created. Each generation of the structure is an era, and old eras stay browsable forever.

Billing account

One invoice-receiving customer: name, locations, contacts, address, optionally its own payment method. Backed 1:1 by a Zenskar customer and a Stripe customer.

billable_entities · identifier immutable, = Zenskar external_id
Structure

Single entity (all locations, one invoice) · Per location (one account each) · Custom groups (you draw the lines). Multi-account structures get a chain-level parent.

chosen_grouping_mode: single | per_location | custom
Parent account

Holds the group's shared card and the Zenskar parent–child links. Bills no locations, never appears in the customer's account list, and is never invoiced (rail 7).

acme_14:::::v3 · billing_parent_links
Era

One structure's lifetime: start, end (or current), and a frozen snapshot of which account billed which location. The invoice history of an era stays reachable after the era closes.

chain_billing_grouping_choices + billing_grouping_entities
Retired / closed

An account from a previous era. Keeps its invoices and its card, settles anything still owed, gets no new invoices. Read-only in the portal.

billable_entities.retired, retired_at
Launch (QC done)

The onboarding milestone after which billing is live. Before it nothing has been metered or invoiced, so setup may wipe and rebuild. After it, only close-and-replace.

initial_onboarding_status = COMPLETED
Identifiers are load-bearing. billable_entities.identifier (shape chain:Name:::) is the Zenskar external_id and is read by six subsystems: zenskar_sync's adopt-first create, the nightly usage push, portal auth scoping, the Redis scope cache, emptied-entity delete, and frontend card writes. It is immutable for the life of the entity. When a new era wants a name a retired row still holds, the system mints a tiebreaker: Acme 14 (v2) in the name slot, acme_14:::::v2 for a chain-level parent. The suffix disambiguates; era membership always comes from tables, never from parsing the string.

Division of labor

  • chain_billing_grouping_choices is bi-temporal: the row with valid_till NULL is the current era. A same-mode regroup still opens a new era on switch (set_mode(force_new_era=True)) because the era key must change even when the mode string does not.
  • billing_parent_links is Loop's source of truth for parent→child. The Zenskar relationship mirror is best-effort and one-directional (a child cannot see its parent in Zenskar, and Zenskar has no relationship delete) — old links stay forever as history; current-tree readers filter on retired = false.
  • bname_module_subscriptions (per location × module, starts at launch) is what the nightly usage push iterates — this is why the pre-launch wipe is safe: before launch there are no active subscriptions, so nothing has accrued.

The system

Parent & child accounts

Why this exists

Multi-account chains wanted one payment method for everything — enter a card once, cover every account. But a Stripe PaymentMethod belongs to exactly one Stripe customer, forever: attaching the same card token to a second account's customer fails, and trying to move it produced the original 409 PM_ON_ANOTHER_ACCOUNT bug that started all of this. You cannot share a method across N customers, so the only correct design is to make the shared method live on exactly one customer that stands for the whole group.

That customer is the parent: a real billing account at chain level whose entire job is to hold the group's card and to represent the family in Zenskar. Children carry no method of their own in shared mode; everything that asks "is this child paid?" consults the parent instead (the coverage cascade on the Payments page).

What it is

  • A real billable_entities row with a chain-level identifier (acme_14::::, later eras acme_14:::::v2, v3…), its own Zenskar customer and Stripe customer.
  • It bills no locations (rail 2 forbids deriving its links from the children), so it never appears in /me/accounts and the portal never lists it as a row — the customer only meets it as "Group payment method · covers all N accounts".
  • It is never invoiced (rail 7): invoices are always raised on children, even when the children have no card. Zenskar autopay cannot charge a child's invoice against the parent's method (no payer override; the Zenskar↔Stripe mapping is 1:1 — verified live), so in shared mode collection happens at the parent, which is workable because Finance owns invoicing.

How it's made

  1. Any multi-account create or switch mints the parent like any other entity (fresh identifier; a previous era's chain-level row is never reused — its Zenskar customer belongs to that era's history).
  2. One billing_parent_links row per child (child unique — a child has at most one parent). This table is Loop's source of truth for the family tree; current-tree readers filter to non-retired children.
  3. The link is mirrored to Zenskar (POST /customer/{parent}/relationships) best-effort: a failure logs for reconcile and never fails the customer's operation. Three Zenskar quirks force this posture: the required relationship_type_id is org-specific and exists only in their dashboard (rail 4 — config, never a constant); the relationship is one-directional (a child cannot see its parent, only the parent lists children); and there is no relationship delete API — which is also why old links are simply kept forever as era history.

When the parent is only an organiser

Some chains (jlc_foods is the live example) keep a parent for grouping and reporting while each child pays its own invoices with its own card. That is the payment_scope = "per_account" opt-out on the era: inheritance switches off everywhere, a cardless child reads its true empty state, and the parent's card pays nobody. The scope carries into new eras on switch.

Invoicing

How an invoice happens

Finance operates invoicing in Zenskar; Loop's job is to make sure the right customers, contracts, and usage exist. The full cycle for one account:

  1. Contract — POST /contract_v2 attaches a plan (phases + pricings) to the Zenskar customer. Dates are ISO; anchor_date sets the cycle. A prepaid plan's first cycle bills $0, so a contract must start one cycle before the first real invoice.
  2. Usage — the nightly push reports each location's metered usage against the account that bills it (identifier-keyed). Subscriptions start at launch.
  3. Generate — POST /invoices/generate_invoice with unix-second from_date/to_date; async, returns invoice_id. Returns 400 INVOICE_ALREADY_GENERATED_FOR_THIS_BILLING_PERIOD once an approved invoice covers the period (a voided one does not block — verified).
  4. Approve — PATCH /invoices/{id}/approve?send_email=false flips draft → approved; a $0 invoice lands directly as paid. Approval is what makes an invoice count for the invoice guard.
  5. Collect — autopay charges the account's default Stripe method; net-30 means an open invoice is the steady state, not an anomaly (no delinquency gate on structure changes).
  6. Void — PATCH /invoices/{id}/void; a voided invoice is cancelled history and is deliberately ignored by the invoice guard, so a mis-issued draft can't freeze an account forever.

The invoice guard

billing/invoice_guard.py answers one question — does this Zenskar customer hold any non-void invoice? — and every delete path asks it first: the pre-launch regroup wipe, single-account delete, and bulk-import discard all 409 when the answer is yes. Fail-closed: a Zenskar read error counts as "invoiced". Keyed on invoices rather than onboarding status because billing-first chains are invoiced before they launch.

Invoicing

Who gets emailed

Invoice emails go to Zenskar contacts on the customer, not to the customer record (which is email-less by design — the dedupe rule above). Both creation paths provision them:

  • First-time setup: after creating the customer, each billing contact from the wizard becomes a Zenskar contact (reuse-first by email; send_invoice honoured). Contacts also get Accounting access to the chain on the Loop side.
  • Versioned switch: the same provisioning runs for every minted customer, best-effort — a Zenskar hiccup logs a reconcile warning rather than failing a committed switch. Parents get no contacts (never invoiced). This closed a real gap: switched accounts originally emailed no one.

Era history

Eras & the versioned switch

The versioned switch is how a live chain changes structure. One POST, all-or-nothing, serialized per chain (advisory lock, 5s wait → 409):

sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  participant C as Customer
  participant W as Wizard (browser)
  participant API as Billing API
  participant DB as Postgres
  participant Z as Zenskar
  C->>W: Continue (new groups defined)
  W->>API: POST /setup/grouping/switch {groups, mode}
  API->>DB: chain lock (5s wait, else 409)
  API->>DB: validate every billed location covered (else 422)
  API->>DB: freeze outgoing era membership (snapshot)
  loop each new account
    API->>DB: mint identifier (natural name, else tiebreaker)
    API->>Z: create customer (email-less) + contacts
  end
  API->>DB: retire old accounts (flag only)
  opt multi-account structure
    API->>DB: mint parent (chain:::::vN), link children
    API->>Z: create parent + relationships (best-effort mirror)
  end
  API->>DB: close era, open new era, snapshot membership
  API->>DB: re-point every location's billing config
  API-->>W: {groups[], parent_billable_entity_id}
  W->>C: payment step for the NEW accounts

Retiring is a flag flip: the old accounts' Zenskar customers, Stripe customers, cards, and dunning all stay live until their balance clears. Only NEW invoice generation stops, via the config re-point. Not done by the switch: ending the closed accounts' Zenskar contracts — a manual Finance step until automated.

Minting rules

  • Natural identifier first; if ANY entity (retired included) holds it, tiebreaker in the name slot; chain-level parents take a 6th segment. IntegrityError → next version; Zenskar tombstone → next version; cap 200 probes / 3 Zenskar retries.
  • Entity insert + Zenskar customer create happen in one savepoint, so a Zenskar refusal rolls back the row and the loop can re-mint cleanly.
  • Never adopt a retired row: reusing it would splice the new era onto the old customer's invoices (rail 3).
  • The create path's case-insensitive display-name conflict check skips retired rows on the switch path — a retired "Acme 14" under acme_14:Acme 14 (v2)::: must not block switching back to that name (this 422'd in testing before the fix).

Era history

The two era tables, with worked rows

Era history rides on exactly two tables with a clean division of labor: choices is the timeline ("what mode, from when to when"), snapshot is each era's frozen membership ("who billed which location during it"). The snapshot's grouping_choice_id is a foreign key to a choices row — that's how they join.

Payments

Payment method model

Two modes, mutually exclusive per era (rail 4):

ModeWhere the method livesHow "is this account paid?" resolves
One method for allOn the chain-level parent (one Stripe customer, one method)Children hold nothing; every read consults the parent. children_inherit_parent_payment_method() is the single writer of the coverage invariant and runs on card save, ACH verify, grouping writes, the realtime refresh, and the daily reconcile.
A method per accountOne per child; parent has noneadditional_config.payment_scope = "per_account" disables inheritance everywhere — a cardless child reads its real (empty) state even when the parent holds a card. Carried into new eras on switch.
  • The portal's per-account payment-methods read falls back to the parent's card for a method-less child in shared scope only — this is what the Autopay column renders, and it deliberately has no retired filter: a closed era's children keep showing the card that settles their remaining balance.
  • ACH goes through micro-deposit verification: an unverified method is never set default (autopay would attempt an uncollectable charge); the verify step finishes it.

Deep dive

The setup wizard's state machine

One component (BillingSetupWizard) serves three mounts — /setup onboarding, /billing/setup review, and the restructure entry — so its hydration logic is where most subtle bugs have lived. The invariants:

Two hydration paths, strictly ordered

  • Local draft first. An unsaved draft in localStorage (keyed per chain: structure, groups, created entities, step, stale accounts, pending-verify count, and switch mode) restores instantly and skips the server resume. A companion effect then fetches payment-state once: if deposits went pending since the draft was written, the draft is dropped (verification owns the flow now) and only accounts still needing a first-time method stay in the form.
  • Server resume otherwise. Three reads — /me/accounts (non-retired only), the grouping (each account's location ids), and payment-state — classify every account: pending_microdeposits → the verify step; none → the payment form (buildNeedPmEntities; these rows carry no Zenskar id, which is why the saved-card view joins back to /me/accounts for one); active → the manage view. All done → finalizeDone marks billing complete exactly once (billingConfirmed stops a revisit from re-PATCHing and re-toasting).
  • A resume identity (the set of account ids) guards re-runs: benign re-renders bail; a CHANGED identity — exactly what a switch produces — re-resumes for the new accounts. This is why cache order matters (below).

Who owns the step

In /setup the parent owns Accounts↔Payment via the URL, so hydration uses hydrateStep (never notifies the parent — a notify here once bounced users to Payment the instant data loaded). The one exception is routePendingOnce: the FIRST hydration that finds pending deposits nudges a parent sitting on Accounts to Payment, once, so the verify code entry isn't hidden a click away.

Rules that prevent re-shipped bugs

  • Stale-draft safety net: if the server has accounts but the local form holds none saved, the create-only groupings POST must not run — it would orphan the live accounts instead of switching them. The wizard drops the draft and re-hydrates (this exact path once orphaned five entities on acme_14).
  • Cache order on any save: REMOVE (not invalidate) the payment-state cache BEFORE invalidating accounts. The accounts invalidation re-runs the resume; against a 60s-warm pre-attach payment-state it re-classified a just-paid account as unpaid and re-mounted the empty card form.
  • onComplete vs onFinish: onComplete re-fires from the resume path on every mount of a done chain — never navigate on it (the restructure page once bounced straight back to /billing on open). onFinish fires only from the customer's own press on a done payment step; hosts navigate on that.
  • Switch responses recover, never resubmit: the switch commits server-side before the response lands; an incomplete response disarms switch mode and re-hydrates from the server rather than re-POSTing.

Deep dive

The account resolver (reuse vs adopt vs create)

First-time setup's create path is non-idempotent, and many chains already have Zenskar customers — Salesforce-era accounts, orphaned Loop-origin rows, a parent from a previous arrangement. The resolver stops the wizard from silently minting duplicates next to them.

  • The probe: on the accounts step the wizard fetches the chain's existing Zenskar profiles, each with its location coverage and the chain's stored grouping mode (which also drives the structure preselect). While it runs, an explicit "checking for existing billing profiles" banner renders — never a silently late chip.
  • Matching: a backend group hint is honored first, then location-coverage overlap. Whole-chain profiles (empty coverage — an unlinked Salesforce customer) are only offered for the single-entity structure; other structures see coverage-bearing profiles only.
  • The parent is one chip. When a parent+children grouping exists, the banner shows the parent as THE handle for the group instead of listing it beside every child as a look-alike peer.
  • Deciding: clicking a banner chip records a reuse decision (and fills the name); pressing Continue with matched-but-undecided groups opens a per-account reuse-vs-create dialog. Decisions ride the payload as reuse_mode / reuse_zenskar_customer_id — reuse links the existing customer, create mints fresh.
  • Name discipline: the create path 422s on a case-insensitive display-name match whose identifier differs (a case-variant would mint a second identifier and orphan the first on reconcile). The switch path relaxes exactly one clause — retired rows don't count — or a chain could never switch back to a name a past era used.
  • The switch skips the resolver entirely: reuse is structurally wrong mid-switch; every entity is minted fresh by design.

Deep dive

ACH micro-deposits

US bank accounts verify by micro-deposit: Stripe sends a $0.01 deposit whose statement descriptor carries a 6-digit code (SM…), which the customer types back 1–3 business days later. Everything in between must hold billing safe:

  1. At save: confirmSetup returns a SetupIntent in requires_action / verify_with_microdeposits. The wizard attaches nothing as default — an unverified method set default would let Zenskar attempt a charge it cannot collect. The customer sees the deposit explainer; autopay explicitly won't charge yet.
  2. While pending: the payment-state classifier scans the Stripe customer's SetupIntents (auto-paging past the first 100 — a pending intent buried behind abandoned attempts still counts) and reports pending_microdeposits with the intent id. The accounts step locks structure changes (restructureLocked) — regrouping mid-verification would strand the deposit — and every resume routes to the verify step once.
  3. Shared mode collapses: one intent on the PARENT covers the whole group, so the verify UI shows one row ("All N accounts") rather than N look-alike rows, and plural copy keys off covered count, not row count.
  4. At verify: the code goes to the backend per entity; success removes the row from pending, drops the 60s payment-method caches (so the bank shows instead of "no payment method on file"), flips parentHoldsMethod when the verified row was the parent, and re-reads payment state. The last verified code completes billing (manage view, or "Billing setup complete" on first-time setup).

Deep dive

Bulk spreadsheet import

Large chains define dozens of accounts at once from a sheet instead of one card at a time. Offered under the structure cards for multi-account structures (Per location / Custom) during setup.

  • Shape: one row = one account — account name, locations (names or numeric ids, resolved server-side), contact name/email. A bad email fails its own row, never the whole file.
  • Reconcile semantics on re-import: the sheet is the source of truth for what it lists. Listed locations MOVE to their listed account; an account the re-import empties is removed — unless it holds a payment method, or the invoice guard says it's invoiced (that row fails instead). Accounts the sheet doesn't mention stay as they are.
  • Hidden in switch mode: on a launched chain the import's delete-based reconcile can only 409 row after row, and it never routes through the versioned switch — so the restructure page doesn't offer it. Bulk restructuring of a live chain is an open follow-up.

The six writes inside that one transaction

  1. Freeze the outgoing era's snapshot rows (if never frozen).
  2. Mint the new accounts — fresh identifiers with (v2)/(v3) tiebreakers — and their Zenskar customers + contacts.
  3. Retire the old accounts: flag only; card and invoices stay.
  4. Choices table: era N gets valid_till = now(), era N+1 inserted open.
  5. Snapshot era N+1's membership.
  6. Re-point every location's bname_billing_config — the write that moves NEW invoices to the new accounts.
Want to see it run on real rows? The dry run of exactly this flow — acme_14's custom → single switch, with the before tables, all the writes, and the after tables — is the One pass: switching acme_14 page.

New invoices change address as a consequence of step one's config re-point: the nightly usage push and Zenskar contracts now bill the new accounts, so the NEXT invoices land under era N+1 while era N's invoices stay exactly where they are — reachable through the dropdown forever.

Reference

The eight rails

1. Never re-home a Stripe PaymentMethod between customers — mint a new one.
2. Never auto-derive a parent's location links from its children.
3. Never insert a second {chain}:::: entity within an era; the versioned switch is the one exception in kind — it retires the era and mints a NEW tiebreakered chain-level entity, because reusing a retired row splices eras onto the wrong invoices.
4. Never hardcode Zenskar's relationship_type_id — it is org-specific, dashboard-only, and lives in config.
5. Never let a non-prod run hold prod Zenskar or live Stripe credentials.
6. Never delete a billing account that has an invoice, on either side. Every delete path runs the invoice guard and 409s; invoiced accounts are only ever RETIRED via the switch.
7. A parent never carries an invoice. It holds the group's card and the relationships; every invoice is raised on a child.
8. The wipe is pre-launch only. After launch every structure change is the versioned switch — nothing is deleted, so there is no invoice/usage check to get right.

Runbooks & gotchas

Local stack

  • Backend: frontend_data_service on :8090 against local Homebrew Postgres (loop_core), prod GCP for BQ/Firestore/auth; FE Vite on :3000 with VITE_OATS_BACKEND_BASE_URL=http://localhost:8090. Launch configs grouping-eras-backend/-frontend.
  • Test chains must have a test_opportunities row with mocked_data=true, or new entities never get a persisted Stripe customer and the payment step 409s ACCOUNT_PROVISIONING forever.
  • Zenskar sandbox seeding: claude_validation/seed_zenskar_invoices.py — sandbox-org-guarded, skips parents, --plan "Basic Prepaid Plan" for real $1,000 invoices (postpaid yields $0 auto-paid), --new-contract to change plans; contracts start one cycle early because the first prepaid cycle bills $0.
  • Local switch parent-mirroring needs ZENSKAR_SANDBOX_PARENT_RELATIONSHIP_TYPE_ID in the sourced env, or links keep zenskar_relationship_id NULL (switch still succeeds; reconcile later).

Gotchas that cost real hours

  • Zenskar dedupes customers by email, not external_id — never put an email on a customer record.
  • Deleted Zenskar customers leave a reserved-name tombstone: invisible to reads, 409 on create, nothing adoptable.
  • generate_invoice takes unix seconds; approved invoices block the period, voided ones don't.
  • React-query staleness around the switch: always remove (not just invalidate) the payment-state cache before invalidating accounts — the resume re-runs on the identity change and will trust whatever cache it finds.
  • eslint-plugin-react-hooks is installed but never registered in this repo — hook-deps mistakes ship silently; review them manually.
